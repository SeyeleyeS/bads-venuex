The problem

Missed inquiries cost cases.

People looking for an injury or DUI lawyer are often stressed and may be contacting more than one firm. When a reply is slow or a call goes unanswered, some move on. Most of these losses have nothing to do with the quality of your legal work. They come from logistics:

  • Slow replies to web forms and emails on busy court days.
  • After-hours messages that sit until morning.
  • Scheduling back-and-forth that stalls before a consultation is booked.
  • No-shows with no follow-up to rebook.
  • Clients going quiet because they haven't heard anything in weeks.

What the concierge does

Steady, careful follow-up, on your terms.

Email follow-up

Acknowledges each new inquiry promptly, then follows up politely if the person goes quiet, with a firm limit on how often.

Approved answers only

Answers common questions (hours, locations, what to bring, how a consultation works) using wording your firm has approved. If there's no approved answer, it doesn't guess.

Scheduling

Offers consultation times, confirms the appointment and sends the details, cutting out the back-and-forth.

Reminders & no-show recovery

Sends reminders before appointments and a kind note with new times after a missed one.

"No news is still news" updates

Proactive check-ins for current clients, based only on the status your team provides, so nobody wonders whether they've been forgotten.

After-hours coverage

Replies to evening and weekend messages, helps book a time and flags everything for your team's morning review.

Urgent or unusual? A person handles it.

Arrests, safety concerns, deadlines, fees, case questions, complaints and anything outside approved answers go straight to the person you name, with a calm holding reply.

FAQ

Questions firms ask

Is Bay Area Media a law firm?

No. Bay Area Media is not a law firm and does not give legal advice. The concierge handles communication logistics; your firm's attorneys stay in control of every legal matter.

What won't the concierge do?

It won't give legal advice, evaluate cases, predict outcomes, discuss fees or retainer terms, or mention deadlines. It won't claim to be an attorney or staff member, and it says it is an automated assistant if asked. Anything like that goes to a person at your firm.

Who sees the messages?

Your firm's designated staff and the Bay Area Media team running your pilot. During the pilot, messages are reviewed before they go out. Every action is logged so your firm can see what was sent and when.

How is client information handled?

The concierge only contacts people who reached out to your firm or are your current clients, and it stops immediately when someone asks. It uses only the information needed to reply and schedule. We'll walk through how data is stored and accessed during the walkthrough so your firm can confirm it fits your own policies and bar rules.

Does it replace our staff?

No. It runs alongside your team, handles routine follow-up and hands anything urgent or unusual to a person.

Which states do you work with?

Injury and DUI firms in California, Nevada and Arizona.
